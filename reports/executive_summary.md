# Executive Summary: Profitability Analysis of APL Logistics

**Prepared by:** Shivansh Mishra

## Purpose
To find which customers, products and markets truly generate profit, and where profit
is being lost, using about 172,000 completed orders from 20,210 customers.

## Headline Results
- The business earned about **31.4 million** in revenue and **3.8 million** in profit,
  a profit margin of **12.1%**.
- **A small group of customers carries the business.** The top 20% of customers produce
  about 79% of profit.
- **About 1 in 5 customers loses money.** Roughly 4,000 customers lost about 1.05 million
  in total, wiping out about 28% of the profit earned elsewhere.
- **Discounts are widespread but do not explain losses.** Nearly every order carries a
  discount, costing about 10% of gross sales, yet deeper discounts do not coincide with
  lower margins.
- **Profit is similar everywhere else.** Markets, customer types and shipping methods all
  earn close to 12%. No product category loses money overall.

## What This Means
Decisions based on sales alone can be misleading. Some customers who bring large sales
actually reduce profit, and the real differences lie between individual customers, not
between regions or customer types.

## Recommended Actions
1. Review the pricing and terms of loss-making customers.
2. Protect the highest-value customers who produce most of the profit.
3. Test a moderate limit on discounts (for example 15% to 20%) on a small group first.
   A 15% limit could add about 400,000 in profit if customers stay.
4. Audit about 6,000 orders that lose more than they sold for, to find the cause.
5. Start recording order dates and cost information so future analysis can find the
   true drivers of profit.

## Cautions
The data has no order dates or cost details, so the estimates assume costs stay the
same and cannot predict how customers would react to changes.
